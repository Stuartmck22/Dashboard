Filming guide two minutes here saves an hour of squinting later

  • One plane, side on. Put the camera perpendicular to the movement — side-on for a serve or smash, front-on for a jump. Angles measured in the camera's plane are the accurate ones.
  • Frame the whole body including feet, with a little headroom, and keep the camera still. A tripod or a wall beats hand-held.
  • Shoot at the highest frame rate you have. 120 or 240 fps slow-mo resolves a contact point; 30 fps does not. Fast shutter if you can set it.
  • Good, even light. Avoid shooting into the sun or through a fence close to the lens.
  • Same setup every time if you want to compare an athlete week to week — camera position is part of the measurement.
